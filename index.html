Open Letter

Open Letter to the University of Sussex in response to its plans for redundancies in Physics and Astronomy

We, the undersigned, write to express our deep concern and opposition to the proposed staff redundancies within the Physics & Astronomy department at the University of Sussex. These cuts are disproportionately impacting Physics & Astronomy within the Faculty of Science, Engineering, and Medicine and focus solely on Particle Physics and Astronomy.

Petition statement

On 28 May 2026 the University opened a consultation proposing to cut around 150 full-time-equivalent posts. Within it, almost every academic in the University's Particle Physics and Astronomy groups has been placed at risk, with a proposed reduction of around 35 per cent of the staff in Experimental Particle Physics, Theoretical Particle Physics, and the Astronomy Center - several times larger, in proportional terms, than the institution-wide average. Singling out Particle Physics and Astronomy on this scale is a choice, not a necessity, and it is the wrong one.

These cuts threaten a department of genuine international standing. It hosts one of the UK's oldest astronomy groups, leads internationally recognised experimental and theoretical work on physics in and beyond the Standard Model and the origin of the matter–antimatter asymmetry of the Universe, leads the effort to build a scalable quantum computer, and plays leading roles in major international collaborations including the ATLAS experiment. The Particle Physics and Astronomy groups at Sussex are a national asset that attracts talent from around the world and supplies graduates skilled in data analysis, computation and machine learning.

Damage on this scale would be lasting and, in large part, irreversible. Reputation built over a generation can be destroyed in far less time; talent lost will not return; and teaching capacity in a resource-intensive subject would be badly stretched.

The University has linked in part the cuts to reductions in STFC funding in Particle Physics, Astronomy and Nuclear Physics, yet STFC's final allocations for this cycle are not yet confirmed; making permanent redundancies now is premature as well as damaging.

We recognise the financial pressures the University faces, but these proposals are not a proportionate response. We call on the University's leadership to rule out compulsory redundancies and work openly with staff, students and stakeholders on sustainable alternatives. And we call on policymakers to provide stable, long-term support for UK science.
